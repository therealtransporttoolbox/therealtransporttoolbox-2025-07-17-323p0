TITLE
The text at 9 pm: why the scheduler, not just the driver, is on the hook under the HVNL

DESCRIPTION
9:15 on a Tuesday night. A driver's phone lights up: "Need you on the 3 am Adelaide. Confirm." He finished at 6:30. On paper the rest fits. Nobody has done the rest of the sum.

This video is about the person who sends that text. Under the Heavy Vehicle National Law a scheduler is anyone who schedules the transport of goods or a driver's work and rest times, whatever their job title. In December 2022 the NSW Supreme Court fined a scheduler $15,000 as an individual, alongside a $180,000 fine for the company and $15,000 for its managing director.

Covered:
- Who counts as a scheduler (HVNL s 5 and s 26A), including the despatch desk and the booking receptionist
- The prohibited request, s 26E, and the "knew or ought to have known" test
- What counts as work under s 221, including engine running in a dock queue
- Standard hours and the 14-day trap
- The sum we recommend before you say yes, and the Adelaide run worked through
- The 6 pm rule for schedule changes, and why the text message is a statutory record
- "Encourage includes give an incentive": on-time bonuses, late deductions and KPIs
