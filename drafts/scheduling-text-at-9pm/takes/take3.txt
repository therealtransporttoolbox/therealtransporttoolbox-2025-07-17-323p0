So what does a lawful schedule look like? It starts with a sum, and the sum is longer than most people think.

Work means more than driving. Section 221 counts loading, unloading, checking the load, refuelling, the pre-start and filling in the diary. And it counts sitting in the driver's seat with the engine running. Two hours creeping forward in a dock queue is two hours of work.

A solo driver on standard hours can work 12 hours in 24 and must take 7 continuous hours of rest. Over 14 days the cap is 144 hours. A driver can be inside 12 hours today and outside 144 this fortnight. If your planner cannot show the 14 day total, you cannot roster lawfully.

Here is the sum we recommend. Distance divided by 75. Add 30 minutes for the pre-start. Add loading and unloading. Add the queue you have measured at that site. Add two 20 minute breaks. Add a 10 percent buffer. If the answer is over 11 hours, the plan needs a rest inside it, a second driver, or a different day. Not a faster drive.
